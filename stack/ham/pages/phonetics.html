Phonetic alphabet (NATO / ITU)

Spellings as published by NATO (nato.int). Note Alfa and Juliett. § 97.119(b)(2) encourages a phonetic alphabet when giving your call sign by voice.

Q-codes used on the ham bands

Meanings from ITU-R Recommendation M.1172 (the international Q code). A Q-code followed by a question mark (on voice, "RQ") asks the question; on its own it gives the answer. Hams also use some loosely, e.g. QRP for low-power operating and QTH for "my location".

QRKHow readable are my signals?Readability is … (1 bad – 5 excellent)
QRLAre you busy?I am busy; please don't interfere. (Hams send "QRL?" to ask if a frequency is in use.)
QRMIs my transmission being interfered with?Yes, interference (1 nil – 5 extreme)
QRNAre you troubled by static?I am troubled by static (1 nil – 5 extreme)
QROShall I increase power?Increase power.
QRPShall I decrease power?Decrease power.
QRQShall I send faster?Send faster.
QRSShall I send more slowly?Send more slowly.
QRTShall I stop sending?Stop sending.
QRUHave you anything for me?I have nothing for you.
QRVAre you ready?I am ready.
QRXWhen will you call me again?I will call you again at … (hams: "stand by")
QRZWho is calling me?You are being called by …
QSAWhat is my signal strength?Your strength is … (1 scarcely perceptible – 5 very good)
QSBAre my signals fading?Your signals are fading.
QSKCan you hear me between your signals?I can hear you between my signals; break in.

RST signal report

The R-S-T system recommended by the ARRL. Voice reports use only R and S ("five nine"); CW reports add T ("599").
